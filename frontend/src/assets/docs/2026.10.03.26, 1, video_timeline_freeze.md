There is an issue in our application where, if a large video file (even 274MB cause problems) is loaded and the timeline playhead is moved, 
the application stops responding to user input for a period of time; specifically, there is a significant delay while all the frames are processed.
This project was ported from a "https://github.com/mifi/lossless-cut" project and the original project does not have this problem.

The attached video demonstrates how the playback marker moves along the timeline (timeline is tall brown area). 
There are two markers: the first features triangles at the top and bottom edges, while the second is simply a line that appears during rapid movement, ensuring the application never freezes.

On the attached screenshot, these two markers are indicated by red rectangles.

**Cursor**

The freeze happens when the playhead moves on a large file, while LosslessCut stays responsive with a separate line marker during fast scrubbing. I’ll compare how this app updates the timeline against that behavior.

The freeze comes from assigning `video.currentTime` on every pointer move, so the decoder works through each intermediate frame. LosslessCut avoids that by moving a triangle playhead immediately and only seeking when the previous frame has finished.

The triangle playhead was being pulled back to the decoded frame. While paused, that position should stay where you dragged it, and only the catch-up line should follow the video.

Dragging the timeline no longer waits for every intermediate frame to decode. The playhead follows the pointer immediately, and the video seeks only to the latest position once the previous frame is ready.

On a large file, each pointer move was setting `video.currentTime`. The decoder then rendered every position along the drag, and the window stopped taking input until that queue finished. LosslessCut avoids this by keeping two markers:

- The marker with triangles at the top and bottom is where you dragged. It moves at once, and the time readout moves with it.
- The plain line is the frame the video has actually decoded. It appears only while that frame is still catching up, then disappears.

A burst of 40 seeks across the 1 hour 43 minute meeting recording (274 MB) finished in 0.3 ms and started 2 video seeks, not 40. The clock jumped to 01:40:38 right away, with the triangle marker near the end of the timeline and the catch-up line still near the beginning.
